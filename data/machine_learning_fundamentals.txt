Machine learning paradigms are broadly categorized into supervised learning, unsupervised learning, and reinforcement learning. Supervised learning trains algorithms on labeled pairs of inputs and ground truth targets, whereas unsupervised learning discovers latent patterns, groupings, and clusters within unlabeled datasets. Reinforcement learning agents optimize sequential decision-making policies through trial-and-error rewards within dynamic environments.

A central challenge in statistical learning is the bias-variance tradeoff. Bias measures the error resulting from overly simplistic model assumptions, leading to underfitting. Variance measures sensitivity to random fluctuations and noise in the training set, leading to overfitting where the model fails to generalize to unseen test distributions.

To mitigate overfitting, regularization techniques constrain parameter complexity. L1 regularization, also known as Lasso regression, introduces a penalty proportional to the absolute values of the weights, driving non-essential coefficients to zero and producing sparse feature representations. In contrast, L2 regularization, known as Ridge regression, penalizes the squared magnitude of weights, shrinking parameters toward zero without setting them strictly to zero.

Model evaluation relies on robust data partitioning. The dataset is partitioned into an 80/10/10 ratio for training, validation, and testing. Cross-validation further protects against data leakage and sampling bias by rotating validation subsets across multiple training folds.
