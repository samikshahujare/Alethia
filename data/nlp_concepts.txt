Natural language processing workflows convert unstructured text into numeric token representations through subword tokenization algorithms. Subword tokenization mitigates out-of-vocabulary problems by decomposing unknown words into frequent subword units. Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) starts with an alphabet of individual characters and iteratively merges the most frequently co-occurring byte pairs into unified vocabulary entries.

WordPiece and SentencePiece build upon subword tokenization principles. WordPiece scores potential token mergers using maximum likelihood estimation rather than raw frequency counts. SentencePiece treats input text as a raw stream of characters, including whitespace characters, allowing seamless language-independent tokenization without pre-segmentation steps.

Language model evaluation employs perplexity as an intrinsic quality metric. Perplexity is mathematically defined as the exponentiated average negative log-likelihood per token, corresponding to the effective branching factor of the model's predictive distribution. Lower perplexity scores indicate higher certainty in predicting ground-truth text tokens.

For generation tasks, downstream evaluation relies on BLEU and ROUGE metrics. The BLEU score computes modified n-gram precision against human reference translations and incorporates a brevity penalty to penalize excessively terse outputs. In contrast, ROUGE focuses on n-gram recall, measuring how much reference information is successfully captured in generated summaries.
