The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system of the Sun and the astronomical celestial objects that orbit it either directly or indirectly. The eight designated planets in order of increasing distance from the Sun are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system, with a mass more than two and a half times that of all other planets combined. Astronomical surveys confirm that Jupiter possesses 95 officially recognized natural moons, including the four large Galilean satellites: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system, even exceeding the planet Mercury in physical diameter.

Mars is known as the Red Planet due to iron oxide prevalent on its surface regolith. Mars hosts Olympus Mons, a massive shield volcano standing approximately 21.9 kilometers high, making it the largest volcano and tallest planetary mountain documented in the solar system.

Saturn is widely distinguished by its extensive and bright planetary ring system. These rings consist predominantly of billions of small water ice particles, ranging in size from micrometers to several meters, accompanied by trace quantities of rocky silicates and tholin organic compounds.
